隱私權政策

吉諾分帳所 Gino's Split Lab
生效日期：2026-10-30

這份政策說明吉諾分帳所（英文名稱 Gino's Split Lab，以下稱「本服務」）存放哪些資料、為什麼存、誰看得到，以及你怎麼匯出或刪除。本服務包含 iPhone App、網頁版 split.ginochang.com，以及兩者共用的伺服器；同一個帳號在 App 和網頁版看到的是同一本帳。

營運者：Gino Chang（法定姓名 Chun-Ju Chang，App Store 上顯示的開發者名稱），個人開發者。聯絡：gino@ginochang.com

重點

  • 只存放記帳、分帳、登入和傳送通知需要的資料。
  • 新帳號需要 Email，用寄到 Email 的驗證碼登入。本服務只寄登入和確認 Email 用的驗證碼，不寄廣告或其他信。
  • 網頁版也可以用 Apple 或 Google 登入（iPhone App 從 1.1 版起也可以）：本服務從 Apple 只拿到 Apple 使用者 ID、Email（或 Apple 替你轉寄的地址）和第一次登入時的名字，從 Google 只拿到你的名字、Email 和 Google 帳戶 ID，拿不到聯絡人、雲端硬碟或其他資料。
  • 沒有廣告，不賣資料，也不追蹤你在其他公司的 App 和網站上的活動。iPhone App 裡沒有任何第三方的分析、廣告或追蹤工具。
  • 你輸入的密碼不會離開你的裝置。
  • 你附在費用上的收據照片（一筆最多三張）會和那筆帳一起存起來，同一個群組的人看得到；掃描收據時，照片也會先附上，儲存前可以移除。
  • 同一個群組的人看得到群組裡的帳，以及你填的收款方式和你選的頭像照片。你的 Email 不會顯示給其他使用者。
  • 隨時可以在 App 或網頁版匯出資料、刪除帳號。

1. 存放哪些資料、為什麼

帳號

  • 名字（必填）：在群組裡顯示給其他成員看。
  • Email（新帳號必填）：用來登入。建立帳號、在新的裝置登入，以及加上或更換 Email 時，本服務會寄一組 6 位數驗證碼到這個 Email，確認它是你的；伺服器記錄這個 Email 是否確認過、在什麼時間確認。別人輸入你的 Email 把你加進群組或一對一帳本時，也用它找到你的帳號（見第 2 節）。本服務只寄登入和確認 Email 用的驗證碼，不寄廣告或其他信。之前只用名字建立的帳號，可以之後在「我的」加上 Email。在有 Email 驗證碼以前建立的帳號，Email 可能沒有人確認過；第一次用驗證碼、Google 或 Apple 證明這個 Email 是你的時，會清除那個帳號原本的密碼，並登出它的其他裝置，因為當初設定的人不一定是這個 Email 的主人。
  • Google 帳戶 ID（只有用 Google 登入或連結過的帳號才有）：你在網頁版或 iPhone App（1.1 版起）按「使用 Google 帳戶登入」時，Google 會告訴本服務你的名字、Email 和一組 Google 帳戶 ID。伺服器存放這個 Email 和 ID，用來認出你；名字只用來當新帳號一開始的顯示名稱（Google 給的名字，沒有的話用全名），之後可以在「我的」改。Google 已經確認過這個 Email，所以它會記成已確認。第一次用 Google 登入時，如果已經有帳號用這個 Email，就會登入那個帳號，並把 Google 帳戶連到它；沒有的話，就建立一個新帳號。你也可以在「我的 › 登入方式」把 Google 帳戶連到已經登入的帳號，Email 不一樣也可以：回到本服務後，會先問你要不要連結那個 Google 帳戶（顯示它的 Email），按「連結」才連上；等你回答的這段時間，伺服器記著它的 ID 和 Email，10 分鐘內有效。如果那個 Google 帳戶在另一個什麼都沒有的帳號上（沒有群組、沒有密碼、沒有其他登入方式），連結時會把它移過來，並刪除那個空帳號。iPhone App 在 iOS 系統的登入視窗打開 Google 的頁面；登入後，伺服器會留一組一次性的代碼，連同 Google 給的名字、Email 和 ID，等 App 用它換登入權杖時才找出或建立帳號（代碼只存雜湊值，5 分鐘內有效、只能用一次，過期的每天清除）。除了這三樣，本服務不向 Google 要任何資料（沒有聯絡人、沒有雲端硬碟），App 和網頁上也不載入 Google 的登入程式。目前不能在 App 或網頁版取消連結；想取消，請寫信給我。
  • Apple 使用者 ID（只有用 Apple 登入或連結過的帳號才有）：你在網頁版或 iPhone App（1.1 版起）按「使用 Apple 登入」時，Apple 會告訴本服務一組 Apple 使用者 ID（Apple 給本服務的固定代號，App 和網頁版是同一組）、一個 Email，以及第一次登入時你的名字。如果你選了「隱藏我的電子郵件」，這個 Email 是 Apple 替你產生的轉寄地址，寄到那裡的信（例如驗證碼）會由 Apple 轉到你的信箱。伺服器存放這個 Email 和 ID，用來認出你；名字只用來當新帳號一開始的顯示名稱（沒有名字的話，用 Email @ 前面的部分，轉寄地址則用「Apple 使用者」），之後可以在「我的」改。Apple 已經確認過這個 Email，所以它會記成已確認。第一次用 Apple 登入時，如果已經有帳號用這個 Email，就會登入那個帳號，並把 Apple 連到它；沒有的話，就建立一個新帳號。你也可以在「我的 › 登入方式」把 Apple 連到已經登入的帳號，Email 不一樣也可以；在網頁版，回到本服務後會先問你要不要連結（顯示它的 Email，或「隱藏的 Email」），等你回答的這段時間，伺服器記著 Apple 使用者 ID、Email 和下面說的授權權杖，10 分鐘內有效；按「取消」或沒有回答，就不連結。如果那個 Apple 帳號在另一個什麼都沒有的帳號上（例如剛才用隱藏 Email 建立的新帳號：沒有群組、沒有密碼、沒有其他登入方式），連結時會把它和它的授權權杖移過來，並刪除那個空帳號。登入時，Apple 也會給本服務一組授權用的權杖（refresh token），伺服器存著它，只用來在你刪除帳號時請 Apple 撤銷本服務的授權，不拿它向 Apple 要其他資料。除了這些，本服務不向 Apple 要任何資料，網頁上也不載入 Apple 的程式。目前不能在 App 或網頁版取消連結；想取消，請寫信給我。
  • 密碼（選填）：App 和網頁版會先用 PBKDF2-SHA256（210,000 次）把你輸入的密碼和 Email 算成一把金鑰，只把這把金鑰送出；伺服器再把它雜湊後存放。伺服器上沒有你的密碼。因為金鑰是跟 Email 一起算的，換成新的 Email 時，原本的密碼會被移除。
  • 頭像顏色、語言、主要幣別（一開始依裝置的語言或地區設定，之後可以改），以及帳號建立和修改的時間。
  • 頭像照片（選填）：你選來當頭像的照片。照片會先在你的裝置上裁成正方形、縮小成 256 x 256 的小圖，伺服器只存這張小圖。
  • 邀請朋友的連結（選填）：你第一次打開「邀請朋友」時，伺服器會替你的帳號產生一組隨機的邀請碼，放在你的連結裡。你可以隨時重設，舊的連結就會失效（見第 2 節）。

收款方式（選填）

  • Venmo 使用者名稱、PayPal.me 使用者名稱，以及「其他收款方式」（最多 300 字，例如 LINE Pay、銀行代碼和帳號）。結清的時候，要付錢給你的朋友會看到這些，方便直接轉給你。

群組和帳目

  • 群組：名稱、類型、幣別、表情符號、顏色、備註、是否簡化債務，以及邀請碼。
  • 成員：名字、顏色、角色（管理員或成員）、是否還在群組。成員可以是有帳號的人，也可以是別人只用名字加進來、還沒人認領的成員。
  • 每一筆費用或還款：說明、分類、金額、幣別、匯率、日期、備註、誰付的、怎麼分、是否重複，以及誰在什麼時間新增或修改。
  • 留言（目前只有網頁版能寫）。
  • 變更紀錄：誰在什麼時間新增、修改、刪除或復原了什麼，包含當時的說明、金額，以及留言和備註的前段文字（最多 200 字）。

登入和使用紀錄

  • 每次登入會建立一個登入工作階段。伺服器只存放登入權杖的 SHA-256 雜湊、裝置類型（例如「iPhone」或「Mac Safari」，從 App 或瀏覽器送出的 User-Agent 判斷）、建立時間、最近使用時間（最多每小時更新一次）和到期時間。你可以在「我的」看到這些裝置，並登出其中任何一台。
  • 你最後一次打開動態頁的時間，用來計算未讀數。
  • 一次性代碼和連結：在其他裝置登入用的登入碼（10 分鐘）、我替你產生的重設密碼連結（30 分鐘）、成員認領連結（7 天）。伺服器只存放它們的雜湊值。
  • Email 驗證碼：寄到你 Email 的 6 位數驗證碼，10 分鐘內有效，最多試 5 次，只能用一次。伺服器只存放驗證碼的雜湊值、收件的 Email、用途（登入，或替帳號加上 Email）、試了幾次，以及建立、到期和使用的時間，紀錄在一天內刪除。

通知（網頁版）

  • 每一台在網頁版開啟通知的裝置：瀏覽器的推播服務給的訂閱網址和兩把金鑰（用來把通知送到這台裝置，並替內容加密）、裝置類型（和登入裝置一樣，從 User-Agent 判斷）、裝置的時區（留給之後的勿擾時段，目前還沒用到）、開啟的時間、最近一次成功送出的時間，以及連續送不出去的次數。這些資料連到開啟通知時的那個登入工作階段。
  • 你選擇要收哪些通知：記帳、還款、有人加入。
  • 通知的內容（群組名稱、誰記了什麼、金額）在伺服器上組好、加密後才交給推播服務，只有你的裝置解得開。同一分鐘要送的通知太多時，一次送不完的會先以文字存在伺服器上，等之後送出時刪除；放了一天還沒送出的，不送就直接刪除。

通知（iPhone App）

  • 每一支在 iPhone App 開啟通知的 iPhone：Apple 推播通知服務（APNs）給這支 iPhone 的裝置代碼（device token，用來把通知送到這支 iPhone）、App 是開發中的版本還是正式版（決定送到 Apple 的哪一台伺服器）、App 的識別碼、App 送來的裝置名稱（例如「iPhone」）、裝置的時區（留給之後的勿擾時段，目前還沒用到）、開啟的時間、最近一次成功送出的時間，以及連續送不出去的次數。這些資料連到開啟通知時的那個登入工作階段。
  • 收哪些通知，和網頁版是同一組選擇：記帳、還款、有人加入。
  • iPhone 上的通知經過 Apple 的推播服務：通知的內容（群組名稱、誰記了什麼、金額）在伺服器上組好，連同裝置代碼交給 Apple，由 Apple 送到你的 iPhone（見第 3 節）。和網頁版不同，這些內容沒有另外加密，Apple 傳送時看得到。同一分鐘要送的通知太多時，一次送不完的會先以文字存在伺服器上，等之後送出時刪除；放了一天還沒送出的，不送就直接刪除。

收據掃描（網頁版和 iPhone App）

  • 新增費用時，可以拍收據或從相簿選照片（一筆最多三張，例如一張很長的收據分段拍），自動填好金額、日期、店名和品項。照片會先在你的裝置上縮小（同時拿掉照片裡的 EXIF 資料，例如拍攝地點）。每掃描一次，這筆費用附上的照片會一起送給 Cloudflare Workers AI 辨識一次（見第 3 節）；送去辨識的那幾份辨識完就丟掉，本服務也不記錄收據上的文字。辨識的結果只填進你正在新增的那一筆，儲存這筆帳之後，才會和其他帳目一樣存起來。
  • 網頁版和 iPhone App（1.1 版起）第一次掃描前會先問你是否同意把收據照片傳給第三方 AI 服務 Cloudflare Workers AI，同意之後才會送出，選「取消」就什麼都不送。這個選擇按帳號記在每台裝置或瀏覽器上，可以在「我的」的「收據掃描」重設，下次掃描前就會再問你。
  • 掃描過的照片會縮小成一份附在這筆費用上（見下面的「收據照片」）；不想留的話，儲存前移除。只用「附收據」附上的照片，要等你掃描時才會送去辨識。

收據照片

  • 你可以把收據照片附在一筆費用上（拍照或從相簿選，或是掃描過的那幾張），同一個群組的人都看得到。照片會先在你的裝置上縮小（最長邊最多 1600 像素），重新存成 JPEG，照片裡的 EXIF 資料（例如拍攝地點）會一起拿掉。伺服器存放這些縮小後的照片（存在 Cloudflare Workers KV，見第 3 節），以及每一張的寬、高、檔案大小、上傳的帳號和上傳時間。一筆費用最多三張。
  • 群組裡的人都可以移除其中任何一張；移除後立刻刪掉。

防止濫用

  • 為了擋下短時間內大量的註冊、登入、用 Google 或 Apple 登入、連結 Google 或 Apple、寄送和輸入 Email 驗證碼、登入碼、重設密碼、設定密碼、刪除帳號時輸入密碼、產生成員認領連結、用 Email 把別人加進群組、用朋友的邀請連結加為朋友、不登入打開邀請朋友的連結、傳送測試通知、上傳頭像照片和收據照片，以及掃描收據的嘗試，伺服器會暫時記錄嘗試次數，也記錄整個服務每天寄出幾封驗證信。記錄的鍵是 IP 位址（IPv6 只取前 64 位元）、Email 的雜湊值，或已登入帳號的 ID。這些紀錄只用來限制次數，不用來判斷你的位置。

匯率

  • 伺服器每天存一份各幣別對美元的匯率表，不含任何個人資料。

你的裝置上

  • iPhone App 把登入權杖存在 iOS 的鑰匙圈（Keychain），並在這支手機上記住一些編輯時的偏好，例如你手動輸入的匯率；1.1 版起也記住每個帳號是否同意掃描時把收據照片傳給 Cloudflare Workers AI。1.1 版起，儲存後還沒上傳完的收據照片會先存在這支手機上（不會放進 iCloud 備份），上傳完、按「不要這張」、登出或刪除帳號時刪掉；如果是伺服器結束了登入（例如在別的裝置登出），照片會留在手機上，同一個帳號再登入時接著上傳，換成別的帳號登入時刪掉。你匯出資料時，App 會在暫存資料夾產生你要分享的檔案。
  • 網頁版用兩個 cookie 記住你的登入，只會送回這個網站：__Host-evenly_session 存登入權杖，設成 HttpOnly，網頁上的程式讀不到；__Host-evenly_user 只存帳號 ID，讓網頁一打開就知道是誰登入。這兩個 cookie 只用來登入，不用來追蹤，最後一次打開網頁版後一年到期。瀏覽器的儲存空間裡還有一份登入權杖的副本、離線用的資料副本，以及在這裡登入過的每個帳號是否同意掃描時把收據照片傳給 Cloudflare Workers AI。
  • 用 Google 登入的那一下（網頁版，以及 iPhone App 打開的系統登入視窗），會再用一個 cookie：__Host-evenly_google，設成 HttpOnly，只存幾個隨機產生的值（用來確認 Google 送回來的結果是給這個瀏覽器的）和登入後要回到的頁面。在「我的」連結 Google 時，要連結的帳號和工作階段由伺服器記下，10 分鐘內有效：Google 送你回來時用掉刪除，沒用到的在每天的清理中刪除。這個 cookie 最多留 10 分鐘，Google 把你送回本服務時就刪除。伺服器的執行紀錄裡不會寫下 Google 給的代碼、權杖或你的 Email。
  • 在網頁版用 Apple 登入的那一下，會用 __Host-evenly_apple，同樣設成 HttpOnly，只存幾個隨機產生的值和登入後要回到的頁面；這個瀏覽器原本登入的帳號和工作階段（在「我的」連結 Apple 時，則是要連結的帳號）由伺服器記下，10 分鐘內有效：Apple 送你回來時用掉刪除，沒用到的在每天的清理中刪除。Apple 是從它自己的網站把結果送回本服務，所以這個 cookie 設成 SameSite=None，才能跟著那一次送回來；它最多留 10 分鐘，Apple 把你送回本服務時就刪除。伺服器的執行紀錄裡不會寫下 Apple 給的代碼、權杖或你的 Email。

2. 誰看得到

  • 同一個群組的成員：群組裡的所有內容（成員名字、帳目和附上的收據照片、留言、變更紀錄、邀請連結），以及群組裡有帳號的成員填的收款方式和選的頭像照片。你的 Email 和密碼不會顯示給其他使用者。
  • 拿到邀請連結的人：不用登入，就能看到群組名稱、表情符號、顏色、幣別、類型、人數，以及成員的名字、顏色和是否已被認領；加入後就看得到整個群組。管理員可以隨時重設邀請連結，舊的連結就會失效。

The server stores only a SHA-256 hash of the session token, the kind of device (such as "iPhone" or "Mac Safari", read from the User-Agent your app or browser sends), when the session was created, when it was last used (updated at most once an hour) and when it expires. Under Account you can see these devices and sign any of them out.
  • When you last opened the activity page, to count unread items.
  • One-time codes and links: sign-in codes for another device (10 minutes), password reset links made for you by the operator (30 minutes) and member claim links (7 days). The server stores only their hashes.
  • Email codes: the 6-digit code emailed to you works for 10 minutes, up to 5 tries, once. The server stores only a hash of the code, the address it went to, what it is for (signing in, or adding the email to an account), how many tries were made, and when it was created, expires and was used. These records are deleted within a day.

Notifications (web app)

  • For each device where you turn on notifications in the web app: the subscription address and the two keys that your browser's push service issues (to deliver notifications to that device and to encrypt them), the kind of device (read from the User-Agent, as for sessions), the device's time zone (kept for quiet hours, which are not built yet), when notifications were turned on, when one was last sent successfully, and how many failed in a row. They are tied to the session that turned notifications on.
  • Which kinds of notification you want: expenses, payments, and someone joining.
  • A notification's text (the group's name, who added what, the amount) is put together on the server and encrypted before it goes to the push service, so only your device can read it. When more notifications are due in a minute than can go out at once, the rest wait on the server as text until a later minute sends them, and are deleted then. One still waiting after a day is deleted without being sent.

Notifications (iPhone app)

  • For each iPhone where you turn on notifications in the iPhone app: the device token that Apple Push Notification service (APNs) issues for that iPhone (to deliver notifications to it), whether the app is a development or a released build (which of Apple's servers to use), the app's identifier, the device name the app sends (such as "iPhone"), the device's time zone (kept for quiet hours, which are not built yet), when notifications were turned on, when one was last sent successfully, and how many failed in a row. They are tied to the session that turned notifications on.
  • Which kinds of notification you want: the same three choices as in the web app (expenses, payments, and someone joining).
  • Notifications on an iPhone go through Apple's push service: the server puts the text together (the group's name, who added what, the amount) and hands it with the device token to Apple, which delivers it to your iPhone (see section 3). Unlike in the web app, the text is not encrypted separately, so Apple can read it while it delivers it. When more notifications are due in a minute than can go out at once, the rest wait on the server as text until a later minute sends them, and are deleted then. One still waiting after a day is deleted without being sent.

Receipt scanning (web app and iPhone app)

  • When you add an expense, you can take or pick photos of a receipt (up to three, for example a long receipt taken in parts) to fill in the amount, date, store and items. The photos are shrunk on your device first, which also removes their EXIF data (such as where they were taken). Each scan sends the expense's photos together, once, to Cloudflare Workers AI to be read (see section 3); the copies sent to be read are dropped once read, and the service does not log the text on the receipt. What was read only fills in the entry you are adding, which is stored like any other entry once you save it.
  • Before your first scan, the web app and the iPhone app (from version 1.1) ask whether you agree to send the receipt photos to Cloudflare Workers AI, a third-party AI service. Nothing is sent until you agree, and Cancel sends nothing. Your answer is kept per account on each device or browser; you can reset it in Receipt scanning under Account, and you will be asked again before your next scan.
  • Scanned photos are also attached to that expense as smaller copies (see Receipt photos below). If you do not want to keep one, remove it before you save. A photo you only attach is sent to be read only when you scan.

Receipt photos

  • You can attach a receipt photo to an expense (take one, pick one from your library, or keep the ones you scanned), and everyone in the group can see it. The photo is shrunk on your device first (at most 1600 pixels on its longest side) and saved again as a JPEG, which removes its EXIF data (such as where it was taken). The server stores those smaller photos (in Cloudflare Workers KV, see section 3) with the width, height and file size of each, the account that uploaded it and when. An expense keeps up to three photos.
  • Anyone in the group can remove any of them; a removed photo is deleted at once.

Abuse prevention

  • To block floods of attempts (signing up, signing in, signing in with Google or Apple, linking Google or Apple, sending and entering email codes, sign-in codes, password resets, setting a password, the password check when deleting an account, making member claim links, adding people to groups by email, accepting friend invite links, opening friend invite links without signing in, sending test notifications, uploading profile photos and receipt photos, and scanning receipts), the server briefly counts them, and it counts how many code emails the whole service sends each day. A count is keyed by an IP address (only the first 64 bits of an IPv6 address), a hash of an email address, or the ID of a signed-in account. These counts are used only to limit attempts, never to work out where you are.

Exchange rates

  • The server keeps one table per day of exchange rates against the US dollar. It contains no personal data.

On your device

  • The iPhone app keeps its session token in the iOS Keychain, and remembers a few editing preferences on the phone, such as an exchange rate you typed by hand, and from version 1.1 whether each account agreed to send receipt photos to Cloudflare Workers AI when it scans. From version 1.1, receipt photos of a save that have not finished uploading are kept on the phone first (never in iCloud backups), and are deleted once they upload, when you tap Discard it, when you sign out, or when you delete the account. If the server ended the session (for example you signed out on another device), they stay on the phone: the same account signing in again uploads them, and another account signing in deletes them. When you export your data, the app writes the file you share to its temporary folder.
  • The web app keeps your sign-in in two cookies that are sent only to this site: __Host-evenly_session holds the session token and is HttpOnly, so scripts on the page cannot read it; __Host-evenly_user holds only your account ID, so the page knows who is signed in as soon as it opens. They are used only to keep you signed in, never for tracking, and expire a year after you last open the web app. Your browser's storage also keeps a copy of the session token, an offline copy of your data, and whether each account signed in here agreed to send receipt photos to Cloudflare Workers AI when it scans.
  • While you sign in with Google (on the web, and in the iOS sign-in window the iPhone app opens), one more cookie is used, __Host-evenly_google. It is HttpOnly and holds only a few random values (to check that Google's answer is meant for this browser) and the page to return to after signing in. When you link Google under Account, the server notes which account and session to link, good for 10 minutes: it is used up when Google sends you back, and one never used is deleted by the daily cleanup. The cookie lasts 10 minutes at most and is deleted when Google sends you back to the service. The server's logs never record the code or tokens Google sends, or your email.
  • While you sign in with Apple on the web, the web app uses __Host-evenly_apple. It is HttpOnly too and holds only a few random values and the page to return to; the account and session this browser was signed in with (when you link Apple under Account, the account to link) are noted on the server, good for 10 minutes: used up when Apple sends you back, and deleted by the daily cleanup if never used. Apple sends its answer back from its own site, so this cookie is SameSite=None, to come along with that answer. It lasts 10 minutes at most and is deleted when Apple sends you back to the service. The server's logs never record the code or tokens Apple sends, or your email.

2. Who can see what

  • Members of the same group see everything in that group (member names, entries and their receipt photos, comments, history and the invite link) and the payment details and profile photos of the group's members who have accounts. Your email and password are never shown to other users.
  • Anyone with a group's invite link can see, without signing in, the group's name, emoji, color, currency, type and size, and its members' names and colors and whether each one is claimed. After joining they see the whole group. Admins can reset the invite link at any time, which stops the old one from working.
  • Anyone with a member claim link sees the group's name and that member's name, and can claim that member.
  • Anyone with your friend invite link can see, without signing in, your name, avatar color and profile photo (the link's preview in a chat app shows your name only, never the photo). Signing in and opening it makes the two of you friends: the service starts your 1:1 ledger (or uses the one you already have) and notifies you, and you each see the other's payment details and profile photo, as members of a group do. You can reset the link under Invite friends at any time, which stops the old one from working; friends you already have are not affected.
  • Anyone who knows your sign-in email can add you to a group or start a 1:1 ledger with you without asking you first, and the members of that group then see your payment details. You can leave a group once your balance in it is settled. After you leave or are removed, you stay in the group as a member who left, and its members still see your current payment details and profile photo, including later changes, until you delete your account. Email me if you want them taken out of those groups sooner.
  • Me, the operator. I can access the database. An owner page that only I can use lists each account's name, email, whether the email is confirmed, whether a password is set, language, currency, when it was created and last used, the kinds of devices signed in, the groups it is in (name, size, number of entries), its payment details and its latest 20 activity items. It never shows password hashes, codes or session tokens. I use it to make password reset links for people who ask, to look into problems, and to see overall usage (such as the number of accounts and how many were used in the last 7 days).

3. Third parties

I don't sell your data or share it with advertisers or data brokers. These services handle some of it:

  • Cloudflare runs the server (Cloudflare Workers, which also serves the web app and this page) and the database (Cloudflare D1), and receipt photos are stored in Cloudflare Workers KV, so everything section 1 lists on the server is stored there. Cloudflare handles your IP address and the content of each request. When you scan, the expense's receipt photos are read by Cloudflare Workers AI (photos that are only attached are never sent to it); according to Cloudflare, it keeps no inputs or outputs of such standard requests once they are processed, apart from a few exceptions such as what the law requires, and does not use them to train AI models.
  • Resend (Resend, Inc., an email delivery service in the United States) sends the code emails for the service. For each one it receives your email address and the message (the code), and processes them only on my instructions, to deliver that email to you (as a processor). The emails carry no tracking image, and the service has open and click tracking turned off.
  • Google sign-in: when you choose to sign in with Google (or link it) in the app or on the web, you sign in and agree on Google's own pages, and Google then gives the service your name, email address and Google account ID. The server contacts Google only during a sign-in (accounts.google.com, oauth2.googleapis.com, and www.googleapis.com for Google's public keys), and neither the app nor the service's pages load Google code. On its own pages Google handles your data independently, not as my processor, and Google's Privacy Policy applies there.
  • Sign in with Apple: when you choose to sign in with Apple (or link it), you sign in and agree on Apple's own screens (the system's sheet on an iPhone, appleid.apple.com on the web), and Apple then gives the service an Apple user ID, an email or forwarding address and, the first time, your name. The server contacts Apple only when you sign in, link Apple or delete your account, and when a link you did not confirm is cleaned up (appleid.apple.com: to trade codes for tokens, for Apple's public keys, and to revoke the authorization), and the service's web pages load no Apple code. If you use Hide My Email, the code emails the service sends you pass through Apple's forwarding service. Apple handles your data independently, not as my processor, and Apple's Privacy Policy applies.
  • GitHub (GitHub Pages) forwards the web app's old address, richlovegod.github.io/evenly/, to split.ginochang.com. When you open the old address, GitHub receives your IP address and browser details.
  • Google Fonts: the web app loads its fonts from Google, so your browser sends your IP address to Google. The iPhone app and this page don't.
  • Push services: when you turn on notifications in the web app, your browser registers the device with its own push service (for example Apple's for Safari, Google's for Chrome, Mozilla's for Firefox and Microsoft's for Edge). The server hands each notification, already encrypted, to that service, which delivers it to your device without being able to read it.
  • Apple Push Notification service (APNs): when you turn on notifications in the iPhone app, your iPhone registers with Apple, and the app gives the service the device token Apple issues. The server hands each notification (the group's name, who added what, the amount) with that token to Apple, which delivers it to your iPhone under its own terms.

  • Venmo and PayPal: only when you tap Venmo or PayPal.me on the settle-up screen, the app opens the other person's payment page, with their username and the amount in the link. What happens in Venmo or PayPal is covered by their privacy policies.
  • Apple: if you choose in your iPhone's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple gives me crash reports and aggregated usage statistics under its own policies.

Cloudflare and Resend process this data only on my instructions and only to provide the service, under their data processing terms with their customers, which protect the data at least as well as this policy does. I don't give your data to anyone else unless the law requires it (for example a court order).

4. How long data is kept

  • Your account, payment details and notification choices: until you delete your account. So are a linked Google account ID and Apple user ID: they are kept until you delete your account (or ask me to unlink them). Apple's authorization token is kept until you delete your account, when it is deleted with the account and the service asks Apple to revoke it.
  • Your profile photo: until you remove or change it under Account, or delete your account; a new photo replaces the old one. Devices that already showed it may keep the old photo in their cache, but the app and the web app no longer show it.
  • What is in a group (members, entries, comments, history) belongs to the whole group and is kept, also after you delete your account (see section 5).
  • Deleted entries, comments and groups are only marked as deleted. Entries can be restored. A deleted comment no longer shows under its entry, but its first 200 characters (all of it, for a short comment) stay in the group's activity feed. Deleted groups are hidden from everyone. The data stays in the database, and nothing removes it automatically for now (attached receipt photos aside, see below). If you are an admin of a deleted group and want it erased for good, email me.
  • Members who leave or are removed stay in the group (marked as no longer in it), so old entries still make sense and balances stay right.
  • Sessions expire 365 days after their last use. Signing out, or removing a device under Account, deletes the session at once; expired ones are deleted daily.
  • Notification subscriptions last until you turn notifications off on that device, that device is signed out or its session expires, or the push service says the subscription is gone. One is also deleted after 5 failed sends in a row. An account keeps at most 10 devices; one more deletes the one that last received a notification longest ago.
  • iPhone device tokens last until you turn notifications off on that iPhone, that iPhone is signed out or its session expires, you delete your account, or Apple says the token is no longer valid. One is also deleted after 5 failed sends in a row. An account keeps at most 10 iPhones; one more deletes the one that last received a notification longest ago.
  • Notifications waiting to go out are deleted when they are sent, and kept a day at most.
  • Each receipt photo attached to an expense is kept until someone removes or replaces it. After the expense is deleted, its photos stay one more day so you can restore it (restoring brings them back), then the daily cleanup deletes them, usually one to two days after the expense was deleted; after a group is deleted, usually within a day. When many are deleted at once, it can take a few more days. Deleting your account does not delete the receipt photos you uploaded: they belong to the group's entries (see section 5). A device that showed a photo may still hold it in its cache, but the app and the web app do not show it again.
  • The copies of receipt photos sent to be scanned are not kept: they are dropped once they have been read.
  • Sign-in codes and links (the one-time codes of the iPhone app's Google sign-in and linking included, which stop working after 5 minutes), the account and session the server notes while you sign in or link with Google or Apple (10 minutes), and a Google account or Apple Account waiting for you to confirm a link (10 minutes) are deleted once used, and by the daily cleanup after they expire (an Apple authorization token that was not linked goes to the account of the service that Apple Account is linked to, if there is one, or else is revoked at Apple).
  • Email codes stop working after 10 minutes, and their records are deleted within a day.
  • Abuse-prevention counts are deleted within about two days after their window (15 minutes to 1 day) ends.
  • Exchange-rate tables are kept; they contain no personal data.

5. Deleting your account

  • In the iPhone app: Account › Delete account. On the web: Delete account at the bottom of the Account page. Accounts with a password confirm it first. You can also email gino@ginochang.com and ask me to do it; include the account's name or email.
  • Deleted: your account (name, email, linked Google account ID and Apple user ID, Apple's authorization token, password hash, payment details, profile photo, color, language, currency, notification choices, the code of your friend invite link), all your sessions, sign-in codes, links waiting for your confirmation, the codes for adding an email to it and notification subscriptions (iPhone device tokens included). Every device is signed out and gets no more notifications. For an account that used Sign in with Apple, the service asks Apple to revoke its authorization as the account is deleted, which normally takes the service off the list of apps using Sign in with Apple in your Apple Account's settings; if Apple does not answer, you can remove it yourself under Settings › your name › Sign-In & Security › Sign in with Apple on an iPhone, or at account.apple.com.
  • Kept: in each group you were in, you become a name-only member that nobody has claimed, and the group's entries, the receipt photos you attached, comments and history stay, so everyone else's balances stay correct. These records no longer point to any account. In a group where you were the only admin, the earliest member who has an account becomes an admin.
  • If you don't want your name to stay in a group either, email me. Renaming yourself in the group first only changes the member's current name: history already written keeps the old one, and the rename itself is recorded.
  • Deletion can't be undone.

6. Export and corrections

  • Account › Export my data in the iPhone app, or Export data on the web app's Account page, makes a JSON file with your account (your profile photo included, and whether a Google account or Apple is linked, never their IDs) and every group you are in, with its members and all its entries (deleted ones included). For each receipt photo the file lists its type, width, height, size and version (a fingerprint of the file), not the photo itself, which you can see on its entry. Comment text and history are not in the file; email me if you want them.
  • You can change your name, profile photo, color, language, currency, payment details, email (confirmed with a code sent to the new address) and password under Account, and remove the photo there too. Groups and entries can be changed inside each group.

7. Security

The app and the web app connect only over HTTPS. The server doesn't have your password, only a hash of the key derived from it, and it stores session tokens only as hashes. Still, no system can promise perfect security.
